## BASICS

### Your castaway
You are the one who reached the cove. Right-click is your command: walk, pick up
what lies on the beach, hand-harvest a tree or a rock, carry it to the fire. You are
not a colonist: you have no job, nobody counts you, raiders do not hunt you, and you
do not eat. If you are beaten down you are knocked out for a few seconds and stand
back up beside the fire.
- Right-click the ground to walk; right-click a stick, a chunk or a crate to fetch it. A green ring marks the click and a trail shows your path.
- Hold Shift and right-click to queue orders. They run one after another, and the label under your name counts what is waiting. A plain right-click drops the queue.
- Right-click a tree, a rock or an ore vein to harvest it by hand. Trees need the Stone Axe, rocks the Stone Pick, ore the Metal Pick. Food is always free to gather.
- Right-click the campfire to deposit what you carry, and to work its queue. Right-click the ground beside it to walk there, dropping what you carry as you pass. Left-click the fire to open its panel.
- Right-click a bench (the fire, a Workshop) to work its queue. Any other command walks you away from it.
- Several pairs of hands share a bench: every idle colonist who comes takes one queued item. Research is one person's work.
- You carry six slots. A full pack sheds byproducts on the ground instead of losing them.
- Space centres the camera on you.

### The day
A day lasts 150 seconds and a night 75; harder difficulties stretch the night. The
dial on the HUD shows where you are in the cycle and the day counter shows the
calendar. Dawn is when the colony decides things: a raid is rolled for the coming
night, and survivors only land by day.
- The sky is rolled at dawn too: sunny, slightly cloudy, partly cloudy or cloudy. Clouds shade whatever they drift over; a cloudy day is dimmer everywhere.
- Colonists work into the evening, then sleep from midnight to dawn: in their hut, or on the ground by the fire if that is where they bunk. Foragers keep close to the fire after dusk and everyone hides in the huts when raiders are near.
- The colony eats once per calendar day, per colonist.
- The run ends at the dawn after the last day, or earlier if you sail away.

### Resources and items
Four pooled resources run the colony: wood, food, stone and metal. Everything else is
an item.
- Sticks and stone chunks lie on the beach; a stick is worth three wood and a chunk three stone when they are deposited or used.
- Crates and barrels from the wreck are salvage: a crate holds six food, a barrel five wood. Workers of the matching job collect them.
- Materials (sticks, chunks) live in the campfire stockpile. Tools stay in your hands. Weapons are made into the stockpile and issued one per warrior.
- The stockpile starts small: sixty items. Storage Pits and Racks and Baskets add room, and so does every Storehouse. Anything over the cap is lost.
- A colonist delivers to the nearest Storehouse or the fire, whichever is closer; everything lands in the one colony store either way.
- A worker carries five of one resource at a time and delivers when full, when night falls, or when raiders are close.

## COLONY

### People
Every survivor has a name and one trait, shown over their head and in the People list
on the campfire panel. The trait moves only the hours they keep; nobody works harder
or eats more for it.
- Steady keeps plain hours: bed at midnight, up at dawn.
- A Night Owl turns in an hour later. An Early Riser is up an hour before dawn. The Hardy do both and get by on four hours.
- The Lazy sleep in an hour past dawn and linger longer between strolls.
- A load in hand is delivered before bed, and a recruit keeps their name as a warrior.

### Survivors
Every so often by day a survivor lands at the cove and walks to the fire, as long as
there is room to house them and nobody is hungry. The campfire houses the first few;
each hut adds room. A warrior takes a bed too.
@colony

### Jobs
A new colonist is idle. Idle colonists are your utility labor: they build whatever
site is waiting, then work any bench with a queue, then patch damaged buildings,
then tidy the beach of loose sticks and salvage. Building and repairing need
Construction to be researched; bench work needs Crafting.
- Give a colonist a job on the campfire panel: wood cutters, foragers, quarriers, miners. Each job opens with its research.
- Pin a colonist to one trade with the Specialists rows: Builders, Crafters, Repairers. A specialist does only that trade and never tidies.
- A job change sends the colonist back to the fire first: they hand in whatever they carry, gear up for the new trade, and only then head out.
- Workers spread themselves across nearby trees and rocks; a crowded node is skipped for a quieter one.
- An unreachable node is left alone for a while; a worker never stands stuck.

### Warriors
A warrior is an idle colonist armed from the stockpile, for a weapon and some food.
They patrol, hold the walls, intercept raiders and heal slowly at the fire. Dismiss
one and the weapon comes back; lose one and it is gone.
- Pick the weapon on the panel before recruiting. The best in stock is the default.
- There is no fixed limit on warriors. Each one keeps their bed and comes from the idle pool, so housing and spare hands are what bound the militia.
- A colonist armed with a bow is an archer: they shoot over walls from a distance and never charge in.
- A warrior with a wooden spear fetches a better spear from the stockpile on their own when there is one and no raid is near.
- A watchtower is eyes, not arms: it sees further than anything else and shows a raid coming, but nobody hits harder for standing near it. Towers that shoot come later, as an upgrade.
- Once you have a warrior, a militia box in the bottom-right corner carries the stance and formation buttons, and the stances are on keys (F5, F8, F9 by default; rebindable).
- The Stance row on the panel gives every warrior one order. Defensive: form a line inside the gate the raiders are coming at (or on the edge of camp without walls), fight what reaches it and walk back to it; spearmen never chase out through the gate, archers shoot over the wall. Offensive: hunt raiders anywhere on the island. Follow: escort your castaway and fight what comes near them. A hurt escort still walks home to heal.
- Warriors going for the same raider come at it from different sides instead of queueing up on one path.
- The Formation row sets how the militia stands when it rallies or escorts: Line (spearmen in front, archers behind), Wedge (a point and two edges, archers inside) or Ring. Auto picks Line, Wedge and Ring for the three stances; Loose is no formation at all.
- On Offensive the militia advances on the raiders together and charges when one is close. Archers keep their distance on Offensive, backing off and shooting; on Defensive and Follow they hold their rank and only step back a pace or two.
- The levy: every spare weapon in the stockpile arms a colonist when raiders reach the fire or lurk on the island. One colonist per weapon (the jobless first, then the nearest) runs home — their own hut, or the fire if that is where they sleep — takes it and fights as a warrior; half a minute after the last threat they walk home again, put it back and go back to the job they had. The Defence line on the campfire panel counts the spare weapons.
- Ring the bell (F10, the Bell row, or the militia box's bottom button) to raise the levy and keep it armed until you silence it. The levy costs only the weapons already in stock: no food, no idle colonist, and they do not make the next raid bigger.
@weapons

### Hunger
Food is eaten a little at a time across the day. When the pool runs dry the colony
goes Hungry: everyone works slower and nobody new lands. A full day without food is
Starving, and one colonist walks back to the cove every day it lasts: the idle
first, then workers, then warriors. Your character never eats.

## RESEARCH

### Research and crafting
Research is learned once per run and unlocks things: jobs, build mode, weapons, the
Workshop, the ship. Recipes are repeatable. Both are queued on a bench, and a queue
only moves while someone stands at it: your character, or an idle colonist. Costs are
taken when the entry finishes; an entry the stockpile cannot yet pay for waits at
the bench rather than failing.
- The campfire teaches the basics and makes anything, slowly. The Workshop makes tools and weapons twice as fast and holds the upgrades; research is the same speed everywhere.
- A research that hands you a tool puts it straight into your hands when it completes, if you are the one at the bench.
- The same research cannot be queued at two benches at once.
- Your character always gets the bench; a colonist waits beside it until you walk away.

## BUILDING

### Placing buildings
Press B for build mode once Construction is researched. A bar appears along the
bottom of the screen with everything you can build, grouped by what it is for; click
a tile or press its number key. A ghost shows where it goes and whether it fits:
green is clear, red is water, a slope, a node or too close to another building.
Placing a building flattens a pad under it and takes the cost at once.
- Walls are drawn: drag across the ground and let go, or click points and double-click (or press Enter). Slants build as slanted walls; end where you began to close a ring. Hold Shift for a square path.
- A gate goes on a straight stretch of wall, never a slanted one.
- G turns a finished wall into a gate. Colonists walk through gates; raiders have to break them.
- Delete or X demolishes what is under the cursor for half the cost back. The campfire cannot be demolished.
- Nothing builds itself. A site advances only while an idle colonist or a Builder works it; up to three can stack, and the second and third each add a full share.
- Damaged buildings are repaired by idle colonists at a quarter of the build cost per full repair, paid as they go.
- Housing starts as a Tent: ten wood and one bed. Left-click any building you own to open its card, and upgrade a tent there into a hut of two beds. Your colonists build the upgrade in place, like any other site, and the tent's bed is gone until they finish.
- The Shipyard only stands on a beach, within a few steps of the water.
- The Storehouse (after Storage Pits) is a drop-off point: build one beside far trees or rock and the colonists working there hand in without the walk home. Right-click it to empty your own hands. It is not a bench; crafting stays at the fire.
@buildings

## RAIDS

### Raids
The island is not empty. From the third day on, each dawn rolls whether raiders come
that night. The chance climbs with every quiet night and a raid is certain after
five of them. The size of a raid is set at the roll: it grows with the day and with
how much you have built, so a rich colony draws a bigger crowd. A banner and the
calendar chip tell you at dawn what is coming.
- Raiders land after dusk and walk in from the far side of the island. The night lasts until the last of them is dead: dawn waits on the raid.
- They go for a gate first, then any warrior in reach, then huts, towers and the Workshop, and break through walls when that is the shorter way. The campfire is the prize; losing it ends the run.
- Colonists hide in the nearest hut while raiders are near and come back out when it is over.
- Warriors heal at the fire, slowly, so a beaten militia needs a quiet night.
@raids
@difficulty

### Neighbours
You are not always the only survivors. If you asked for rivals when you started the
run, another ship breaks up on a far shore about a third of the way through, and its
people found a colony of their own. A banner tells you it happened; where they landed
is not marked, and their camp stays hidden until your own people walk far enough to
see it.
- They work their own patch of the island and only reach into yours once their side of it is bare.
- They are not raiders. They start neutral, defend what is theirs, and grow on their own:
  research, huts, a wall, spears and warriors, each camp with a temperament of its own.
- Raiders are everyone's problem. The dawn warning names whose shore they make for; the
  richer camp draws them more often.
- What they think of you is a word on the DIPLOMACY screen (Esc, or the Neighbours entry
  on the bar once you have met them). Warriors on their side of the shared ground (nearer their fire than yours) or blows landed cool it;
  quiet days and peace warm it. Cool enough for two days and they turn hostile, warm
  enough and they become allies.
- Propose peace with a gift from your stores, once a day. Declare war and it is war at
  once - and the other colonies think less of you for it.
- A hostile camp with warriors to spare lands a party on your shore by day to fight your
  militia; it sails home at dawn. An ally whose own fire is quiet sends half its warriors
  to yours when raiders are at it, and you can see their camp on the minimap.

### The endings
Hold out to the dawn after the last day and a passing sail finds the fire. Or
research Shipwright, build the Shipyard on a beach, and click it to set sail early
with everyone aboard. Both are victories; the ship is the better one.
